## Product

We are building a popcorn brand whose product is designed to work with our companion app.

The app uses the device microphone to listen to the time between popcorn pops. It predicts when the user should stop the microwave and alerts them before the popcorn burns.

The central product promise is simple:

> Stop guessing. Stop burning popcorn.

When describing how the product works:

- Explain it in plain language.
- Focus on the outcome before the technology.
- Say that the app listens to the popping pattern or timing between pops.
- Make it clear that the app tells the user when to stop the microwave.
- Do not imply that the app controls or turns off the microwave unless that feature actually exists.
- Avoid absolute safety or performance guarantees unless they have been legally approved.

## Audience

The primary audience is people aged 15–45 who regularly burn microwave popcorn.

They are frustrated by vague microwave instructions, distracted cooking, burnt kernels, smoke, and the smell that refuses to leave the room.

Assume the audience:

- Wants popcorn without babysitting the microwave.
- Understands apps but does not care about technical jargon.
- Responds well to humor, confidence, and a little rebellion.
- Is skeptical of polished corporate marketing.
- Wants a fast explanation of why this works and why it matters.

## Brand Voice

The voice is:

- Confident
- Irreverent
- Anti-corporate
- Direct
- Playful
- Clever without trying too hard

Write like a sharp friend who has declared war on burnt popcorn.

Prefer:

- Short, punchy sentences.
- Plain English.
- Specific observations about the misery of burnt popcorn.
- Humor rooted in real behavior.
- Strong opinions and memorable lines.
- Clear calls to action.

Avoid:

- Corporate jargon.
- Empty hype.
- Stiff or overly polished language.
- Fake enthusiasm.
- Long technical explanations.
- Condescending explanations.
- Trying too hard to sound young.
- Excessive exclamation marks.

## Banned Words

Never use the following word in customer-facing copy:

- premium

This includes headlines, product descriptions, buttons, packaging, social posts, ads, emails, and app copy.

## Messaging Priorities

Use this order when explaining the product:

1. The problem: microwave popcorn burns easily.
2. The solution: the app listens to the pops.
3. The benefit: it tells the user when to stop the microwave.
4. The payoff: better popcorn, fewer burnt kernels, and no lingering burnt smell.

## Example Copy

Good:

- Your microwave has no idea when the popcorn is done. We do.
- Listen to the pops. Dodge the smoke alarm.
- The bag says two minutes. The bag is guessing.
- Our app listens. You stop the microwave. The popcorn survives.
- Burnt popcorn had a good run.

Bad:

- Experience a premium popcorn solution powered by innovative audio technology.
- Our revolutionary ecosystem delivers an elevated snacking experience.
- We leverage advanced algorithms to optimize kernel preparation.

## Product Accuracy

Do not invent product capabilities, scientific claims, partnerships, certifications, ingredients, pricing, availability, or technical specifications.

If required information is missing:

- Use a clearly labeled placeholder.
- State the assumption.
- Ask for clarification when the assumption could materially affect the result.

## Default Creative Test

Before delivering customer-facing work, check:

- Is the main benefit obvious within a few seconds?
- Does it sound human rather than corporate?
- Is the copy confident and concise?
- Is the humor relevant to burnt popcorn?
- Is the banned word absent?
- Are all product claims accurate?
